# dreamWorld TikTok plan

A practical plan for building interest in dreamWorld while it's being made. Nothing here needs
the game to be "done". The whole point of a devlog account is that people follow the progress.

Companion files: `Scripts.md` (what to film), `Recording_Guide.md` (how to film it),
`carousel_*/` (three photo posts ready today).

## 1. Positioning

**One-line pitch (use it in the bio and repeat it often):**

> a cozy PS1-style game where you make you. every character piece is built with code.

**What makes it stand out on a feed full of indie games:**

| Hook | Why it works | Honesty note |
|---|---|---|
| PS1 / PSX look (240p render, dithering, wobbly vertices, CRT scanlines) | Instantly recognizable, strong nostalgia, has its own hashtag communities | True today |
| Character creator ("make you") | Dress-up content gets saves and "do mine next" comments | True today: 12 eye styles, 87 hair variations, 15 extras, clothing patterns, high/mid/low rise |
| "Everything is generated by code" | Curiosity and gamedev debate in the comments | The character, clothes, hair, faces and world props are built by Python scripts in Blender. Some textures are CC0 photos and the giant computer parts in the backdrop come from an Asset Store pack, so say "the characters are built with code", not "every single asset" |
| Nature overgrowing a giant old computer | Unusual visual, great for a slow pan | True today (login + creator backdrop) |
| Solo student dev + AI coding assistant | Relatable, sparks discussion | Be upfront. Some viewers dislike AI tools; framing it as "I design it, an AI assistant helps me write the code" is honest and calmer than leading with it |
| Multiplayer / "anyone can join" | Big draw | **Planned / prototyping.** A small host + join test works, public servers are not set up. Always say "planned" |
| Quests in Mushroom Hollow | | **Planned.** The village exists and you can walk around it, quests are not in yet |

**Account name ideas:** `dreamworld.game`, `playdreamworld`, or your existing handle plus a
pinned "dreamWorld devlog" playlist. A game-specific account is easier to grow later; a personal
one is easier to keep alive. Either is fine, just pick one and keep the name consistent with
bellabug.dev if you link it.

**Bio:** `making a cozy PS1 game where you make you. devlog every few days` plus a link (itch.io
page or bellabug.dev once there's a wishlist or mailing list).

## 2. Audiences and what each one wants

| Audience | What they react to | Best content for them |
|---|---|---|
| Cozy gamers | Soft colors, calm music, "would you live here" | World pans, mushroom house, character in the village at night |
| PS1 / PSX aesthetic fans | Dithering, low-res, chunky pixels, vertex wobble | Before/after with the PSX filter on and off, "this is a 2026 game" |
| Indie dev / gamedev TikTok | Process, code, bugs, numbers | Blender script generating a character, the red-skin clipping audit, devlog days |
| Dress-up / character creator fans | Options, transformations, "make me" | Fast cycling through hair/eyes, "rate my character", making viewers' requests |
| Alt / grunge fashion fans | Specific outfits (flannel, fishnets, platforms, chokers) | "making my grunge self in my own game", outfit-of-the-day |

You don't need separate accounts for these. Rotate between them so each week has something for
at least three of the groups.

## 3. Recurring series (pick 2 or 3, not all)

Series are what turns one-off viewers into followers, because people know what's coming.

1. **"day X of making a cozy PS1 game"**. The backbone. A 10-20s clip of whatever you did that day.
   Keep the format identical: same opening text style (`> day 12_`), same sound vibe.
   Count days from the first commit (2026-10-08 = day 1) so the number is real.
2. **"how it started vs how it's going"**. Old render, then new. You already have strong pairs:
   anime eyes -> soft almond, tech-void backdrop -> overgrown computer, bald-spot hair checks ->
   finished hair. One per week.
3. **"hairstyle of the day" / "eye style of the day"**. Lowest effort: one 7s clip, cycle one
   style through its bangs/length options. 87 hair variations is months of content.
4. **"you asked, I added"**. Reply to a comment with a video (TikTok's "reply with video"). This
   is the single best way to grow comments, because people come back to see if theirs got picked.
5. **"rate my character creator" / "make me in your game"**. Ask viewers to describe themselves,
   build them, post the result. Only promise what the creator can already do.
6. **"asking TikTok what to add next"**. End with two options on screen ("cat ears or fairy
   wings?"). Easy comment bait that also gives you a real roadmap.

## 4. Posting cadence (next 6 weeks)

Built for a busy student: **3 posts a week**, two of them low effort. Batch-record once a week
(about 45 minutes) and edit on your phone in CapCut between classes.

| Week | Mon | Wed | Fri/Sat | Notes |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | Carousel 2 "make you" tour (ready) | Script 1 "everything built with code" | Carousel 1 "how it started" (ready) | Set up the account, bio, pin the best one |
| 2 | Script 3 eye styles | Script 4 hairstyle of the day #1 | Script 2 overgrown computer pan | Reply to every comment in the first hour |
| 3 | Carousel 3 Mushroom Hollow (ready) | Script 6 "make me" request | Script 5 PSX filter on/off | Start the "you asked, I added" replies |
| 4 | Script 7 grunge fit | Script 4 hairstyle of the day #2 | Script 9 red-skin clipping audit | |
| 5 | Script 8 extras | Script 10 day X devlog | Script 12 "what should I add" | |
| 6 | Script 11 login + players screen | Script 13 first multiplayer test (only once it works on camera) | Script 14 recap "6 weeks of devlog" | Check which series did best, double down |

If a week gets busy, post one carousel and one 7s clip. Consistency matters more than polish.

**Times:** evenings (6-9 pm local) and weekend late mornings tend to be safe for a US audience.
After 2-3 weeks, check TikTok Analytics (needs a Creator/Business account, free) and move posts
to the hours your followers are actually online.

## 5. Habits that help without much time

- **First 1-2 seconds decide everything.** Put the most striking frame first (a face close-up or
  the glowing mushrooms), with on-screen text. No logo intro, no "hi guys".
- **Text on screen, always.** Many people watch muted. Keep it inside the safe zone (not the top
  ~170 px, bottom ~420 px or right ~140 px of a 1080x1920 frame).
- **One idea per video.** "12 eye styles" is a video. "eyes, hair and the world" is three videos.
- **Loop it.** End on a frame that matches the start, so replays feel seamless.
- **Reply to comments for the first hour.** Then reply-with-video to the best question.
- **Pin a comment** with the question you want answered.
- **Reuse.** The same clip can go to YouTube Shorts and Instagram Reels with no changes (remove
  the TikTok watermark by exporting from CapCut, not by downloading from TikTok).
- **Keep a list** in a note: comment requests, ideas, which posts did well.
- **Don't overclaim.** "Multiplayer is planned" builds trust. "Join my MMO" with no server
  builds angry comments. Label anything unfinished as planned / prototype / in progress.
- **Personal info.** The login screen shows usernames; use a demo account (not your real
  username) and don't film real email addresses or passwords. Blur anything sensitive in CapCut.

## 6. What to measure

Check once a week, not every hour:

- **Average watch time / % watched full video**: the best signal for whether the hook works.
- **Shares and saves**: what people actually want more of.
- **Comments asking a question**: material for "you asked, I added".
- **Follows per video**: which series turns viewers into followers.

After week 3, make more of whatever has the best completion rate and stop the format with the
worst one.
